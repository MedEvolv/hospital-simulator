# Institutional Mirror Systems Ontology

## Purpose

This ontology defines the core systems language of Institutional Mirror.

The goal is precision. These terms should not drift into slogans. Each concept below has a specific role in the simulator, a specific failure mode it reveals, and a boundary around what it is not.

Institutional Mirror models hospitals as operational, ethical, cognitive, and adaptive systems under pressure. This ontology keeps those layers coherent.

---

## 1. Operational Trust

### Plain-English Definition

Operational Trust is the sustained ability of patients, staff, operators, and the institution to rely on the system under uncertainty, overload, drift, and imperfect information.

It is not sentiment. It is a stability variable.

### What Increases It

- transparent escalation
- explained prioritisation
- timely intervention
- fair queue movement
- successful overload recovery
- visible governance response
- completed accountability traces
- staff seeing that escalation produces action
- patients receiving status updates during delay

### What Decreases It

- unexplained delays
- unexplained queue reordering
- escalation failures
- opaque AI or policy prioritisation
- avoidable harm
- fairness drift
- repeated reversals
- unavailable human oversight
- workload imbalance
- visible throughput gains paired with dignity loss

### What It Affects

- patient compliance
- patient abandonment risk
- complaint amplification
- staff escalation willingness
- workflow bypass probability
- procedural compliance
- institutional fragility
- willingness to rely on AI-mediated operations

### What It Is Not

- patient satisfaction
- brand reputation
- staff morale by itself
- a public trust index
- a score for ranking hospitals
- proof that a decision was correct

## 2. Ethical Debt

### Plain-English Definition

Ethical Debt is unresolved moral weight accumulated through repeated operational compromises.

It represents harm, dignity loss, fairness drift, or unresolved value conflict that remains inside the institution even when the immediate operational event has passed.

### What Increases It

- avoidable harm
- repeated unexplained overrides
- sustained staff stress
- dignity violations
- policy promises without follow-through
- fairness drift
- hidden or unreviewed escalation failures
- patients waiting without communication
- normalisation of overload

### What Decreases It

- explicit governance review
- corrective policy changes
- transparent acknowledgment of tradeoffs
- resourcing changes that reduce recurring compromise
- successful fairness interventions
- visible closure of escalation loops

### What It Affects

- institutional trust
- governance stability
- moral injury pressure
- future escalation willingness
- hidden strain
- institutional brittleness
- reflective insight severity

### What It Is Not

- legal liability
- a blame ledger
- a moral ranking
- proof of negligence
- a substitute for clinical audit

---

## 3. Governance Drift

### Plain-English Definition

Governance Drift is the gap that opens when declared policies, escalation pathways, or safeguards remain formally present but become weaker in actual operational behaviour.

The policy still exists. The institution has started behaving around it.

### What Increases It

- frequent exceptions becoming routine
- overrides without explanation
- escalation suggestions without action
- fairness safeguards not firing under load
- policy adherence declining during overload
- staff workarounds replacing formal procedure
- accountability traces becoming incomplete
- throughput preserved while transparency declines

### What Decreases It

- documented interventions
- clear exception handling
- active fairness safeguards
- escalation paths resolving bottlenecks
- policy review after repeated drift
- transparent human override records

### What It Affects

- governance stability
- operational trust
- ethical debt
- hidden strain
- procedural compliance
- institutional self-awareness

### What It Is Not

- policy failure in the abstract
- regulatory non-compliance by itself
- proof that staff acted wrongly
- ordinary operational flexibility

---

## 4. Hidden Strain

### Plain-English Definition

Hidden Strain is unresolved operational pressure that has not yet appeared as a visible failure but is already changing institutional behaviour.

It is the accumulation before collapse.

### What Increases It

- queues hovering near threshold
- sustained room saturation
- staff stress without relief
- ethical debt rising without response
- repeated tension signals
- patients waiting without status updates
- escalation congestion
- overload treated as normal
- unresolved bottlenecks shifting downstream

### What Decreases It

- real queue pressure relief
- staffing support
- room decompression
- transparent patient communication
- successful escalation
- governance intervention that addresses the source condition
- recovery periods after overload

### What It Affects

- delayed failure risk
- staff burnout
- patient trust
- workflow bypass probability
- normalised dysfunction
- institutional fatigue memory
- reflective alert severity

### What It Is Not

- visible queue length alone
- current stress alone
- a simple capacity metric
- dramatic crisis
- always visible to administrators

---

## 5. Institutional Cognition

### Plain-English Definition

Institutional Cognition is the institution's practical ability to perceive, interpret, remember, and respond to its own operational state.

It is not consciousness. It is the institution's sensing-and-response capacity.

### What Increases It

- complete event logs
- transparent decision traces
- reflective insights
- governance review loops
- historical memory of overload
- reliable escalation feedback
- explicit tradeoff reporting
- staff and patient signals being captured

### What Decreases It

- missing event traces
- invisible workarounds
- hidden harms
- suppressed escalation
- fragmented data
- unreviewed ethical debt
- governance washing
- operational overload that narrows perception

### What It Affects

- ability to learn from simulation
- quality of governance response
- prevention of institutional self-deception
- adaptation under stress
- trust recovery
- policy refinement

### What It Is Not

- AI intelligence
- a chatbot capability
- managerial awareness alone
- more dashboards
- perfect institutional self-knowledge

---

## 6. Escalation Congestion

### Plain-English Definition

Escalation Congestion is the buildup of unresolved issues that require higher-level attention, human review, or governance intervention faster than the institution can process them.

It is the governance equivalent of a queue.

### What Increases It

- more escalation suggestions
- more refusals requiring human oversight
- more ethical conflicts
- overloaded supervisors
- unclear ownership
- delayed review
- repeated policy exceptions
- unavailable senior decision-makers

### What Decreases It

- clear escalation ownership
- timely human review
- decision rights mapped in advance
- escalation triage
- governance staffing
- closure records
- fewer ambiguous policy conflicts

### What It Affects

- operational trust
- hidden strain
- governance drift
- staff escalation willingness
- delayed harm emergence
- institutional fragility

### What It Is Not

- clinical triage queue length
- number of alerts alone
- proof that escalation is bad
- a reason to suppress escalation

---

## 7. Reflective Awareness

### Plain-English Definition

Reflective Awareness is the system's ability to surface what institutional patterns are emerging from operational events.

It answers: what does this behaviour reveal about the institution under pressure?

### What Increases It

- linked event evidence
- causal traces
- moral reckoning outputs
- trust and strain trends
- governance observations
- historical comparison
- explicit uncertainty
- refusal visibility

### What Decreases It

- single-score summaries
- disconnected metrics
- missing event context
- hidden assumptions
- governance washing
- alert floods
- outputs framed as verdicts rather than observations

### What It Affects

- quality of governance conversations
- stakeholder understanding
- institutional learning
- ability to distinguish forced harm from avoidable harm
- prevention of self-deception

### What It Is Not

- generic analytics
- final judgement
- compliance certification
- moral certainty
- a replacement for human review

---

## 8. Normalized Dysfunction

### Plain-English Definition

Normalized Dysfunction is a harmful operational pattern that has repeated often enough that the institution stops treating it as abnormal.

It is not crisis. It is crisis becoming routine.

### What Increases It

- repeated overload without intervention
- repeated corridor waits
- recurring unexplained reorders
- repeated escalation failures
- staff adapting through informal workarounds
- patients absorbing delay without recorded complaint
- ethical debt increasing without governance response

### What Decreases It

- naming the pattern explicitly
- governance review
- policy correction
- resourcing correction
- visible escalation closure
- trend interruption
- staff acknowledgement and support

### What It Affects

- hidden strain
- governance drift
- operational trust
- staff moral injury
- patient dignity
- institutional cognition

### What It Is Not

- adaptation in general
- resilience
- staff professionalism
- evidence that the institution is coping well

---

## 9. Institutional Fatigue Memory

### Plain-English Definition

Institutional Fatigue Memory is the lingering effect of prior overload on future behaviour, even after immediate pressure appears to have reduced.

The queue may clear, but the institution has not fully recovered.

### What Increases It

- sustained high-acuity load
- repeated unresolved escalations
- long periods of staff stress
- rapid cycles of overload and partial recovery
- recurring hidden strain
- repeated ethical compromise

### What Decreases It

- genuine recovery periods
- staffing relief
- closure of unresolved events
- transparent post-event review
- reduction in hidden strain
- trust-restoring intervention

### What It Affects

- future escalation willingness
- staff fatigue baseline
- procedural compliance
- response time
- bypass probability
- governance stability
- trust recovery rate

### What It Is Not

- individual memory
- ordinary fatigue alone
- historical data archive
- proof of permanent damage

## 11. Language Rules

Use:

- "A signal was detected..."
- "The simulation observed..."
- "This may indicate..."
- "This is useful for governance review..."
- "Structural pressure appears to be..."

Avoid:

- "The hospital failed..."
- "Staff caused..."
- "This proves..."
- "Compliance score..."
- "Governance grade..."
- "The correct answer is..."

## 12. Implementation Boundary

The ontology defines concepts. It does not decide clinical truth.

Engine modules may compute derived indicators using these concepts, but every output must remain:

- deterministic
- inspectable
- non-accusatory
- explicitly bounded
- tied to event evidence
- reviewable by humans
