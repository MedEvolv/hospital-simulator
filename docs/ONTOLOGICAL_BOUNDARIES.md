# ONTOLOGICAL BOUNDARIES - NON-NEGOTIABLE

## What This System IS

This system is:

### 1. A Stance Toward Decision-Making Under Irreducible Uncertainty
- Acknowledges that some tensions cannot be resolved, only managed
- Refuses to collapse complexity into false simplicity
- Preserves awareness of trade-offs rather than hiding them

### 2. A Refusal of Premature Closure
- Does not claim to know "the right answer"
- Does not optimize values into weights without remainder
- Does not pretend unavoidable harm can be eliminated

### 3. A Commitment to Visible Tension Over False Resolution
- Makes ethical strain explicit and cumulative
- Surfaces gaps between declared values and observed behavior
- Names loss and moral cost instead of erasing them

### 4. A Mirror for Institutional Self-Awareness
- Helps institutions see how they actually behave under pressure
- Detects value drift between stated principles and operational reality
- Prevents self-deception about moral costs being paid

### 5. A Tool for Institutional Moral Reckoning
- Tracks ethical debt that accumulates from repeated compromises
- Distinguishes forced harm from capacity-induced harm
- Produces honest accounting of unavoidable costs

---

## What This System IS NOT

This system is explicitly NOT:

### ❌ NOT a Rules Engine
- Does not execute deterministic if-then logic
- Does not claim algorithmic certainty
- Preserves space for human judgment

### ❌ NOT an Optimizer
- Does not maximize metrics
- Does not collapse ethics into penalties
- Resists turning dignity into "tolerable deviation"

### ❌ NOT a Value Function
- Does not reduce moral considerations to numerical scores
- Does not claim to compute "the right choice"
- Refuses to flatten asymmetric values

### ❌ NOT a Performance Ranking Tool
- Does not compare institutions for competitive ranking
- Does not produce league tables
- Does not gamify care delivery

### ❌ NOT a Justification Engine for Austerity
- Does not legitimize under-resourcing
- Does not normalize capacity-induced harm as inevitable
- Explicitly flags when harm is avoidable with resources

### ❌ NOT a Predictive Triage System
- Does not claim to predict clinical outcomes
- Does not automate triage decisions
- Does not replace clinical judgment

### ❌ NOT a Sales Demo for Efficiency Gains
- Does not celebrate throughput optimization
- Does not treat speed as inherently good
- Does not hide costs of efficiency

### ❌ NOT an AI Decision-Maker
- Does not make autonomous clinical choices
- Does not claim superior judgment to humans
- Explicitly defers to human authority

---

## If Someone Asks For These Features

**The answer is explicitly NO.**

### Common Requests We Refuse:

**Request:** "Can we use this to rank hospitals by performance?"  
**Answer:** No. This system surfaces trade-offs, not performance hierarchies.

**Request:** "Can we optimize to reduce ethical override counts?"  
**Answer:** No. High override counts may indicate proper response to danger, not failure.

**Request:** "Can we train an ML model on this to predict best actions?"  
**Answer:** No. This would collapse visible tension into hidden optimization.

**Request:** "Can we use this to justify staffing cuts?"  
**Answer:** No. The system explicitly flags capacity-induced harm as avoidable.

**Request:** "Can we add predictive triage to reduce wait times?"  
**Answer:** No. Clinical decisions remain with clinicians.

**Request:** "Can we make this an autonomous agent?"  
**Answer:** No. Human override must always be possible and prominent.

## Why These Boundaries Matter

### They Prevent:
1. **Optimization Theater** - Looking good on metrics while hiding moral costs
2. **Value Collapse** - Reducing ethics to weights and penalties
3. **Institutional Self-Deception** - Claiming success while absorbing harm
4. **Moral Drift** - Losing sight of stated values under pressure
5. **Austerity Justification** - Normalizing avoidable harm as "efficient"

### They Preserve:
1. **Epistemic Humility** - Acknowledging what we don't and can't know
2. **Moral Vocabulary** - Keeping language of dignity, fairness, harm
3. **Visible Tension** - Showing incompatible goods that must be balanced
4. **Human Authority** - Ensuring decisions remain with people, not algorithms
5. **Institutional Honesty** - Forcing confrontation with actual behavior

## How to Protect These Boundaries

### In Documentation:
- State these limits explicitly in all materials
- Repeat them in demos and presentations
- Include in technical specifications

### In Code:
- No functions named "optimize_outcome"
- No metrics called "performance_score"
- No features for comparative ranking
- Explicit "system refused" states when uncertain

### In Demos:
- Show uncomfortable truths, not impressive metrics
- Highlight costs, not just capabilities
- Emphasize what system does NOT do

### In Conversations:
- When someone asks for forbidden features: "That would violate the system's purpose."
- When someone wants optimization: "This system makes trade-offs visible, not optimal."
- When someone wants prediction: "That would hide uncertainty instead of showing it."
